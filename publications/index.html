2026

  1. arXiv 2026
    Last Translation Benchmark
    Vilém Zouhar, Niyati Bafna, Mukund Choudhary, and 4 more authors
    2026
    Abs arXiv Bib Code Demo

    Machine translation benchmarks are often either too easy or too artificial, and automatic metrics grow less reliable as models improve. The Last Translation Benchmark collects human-authored, peer-reviewed examples (text, images, audio, video) that break state-of-the-art MT systems, each with handcrafted verification rules for objective evaluation. It is a live dataset open to contributions.

  2. ICML-W 2026
    Who Flips? Self- and Cross-Model Counterarguments Reveal Answer Instability in LLMs
    Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Amir Hossein Kargaran, Shaghayegh Kolli, and 1 more author
    In ICML 2026 Workshops: Agents in the Wild (AIWILD); Trustworthy AI for Good (AI4GOOD), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib Code

    Accuracy benchmarks check whether LLMs reach the right answer, not whether they keep it. We challenge correct answers with a coherent argument for a wrong option and measure how often models flip. Across seven frontier models and 57 MMLU subjects, flip rates range from 17.5% to 97.3%; self-attribution raises them further, and our cross-model MaxFlip benchmark amplifies flips by up to 23.6pp.

  3. ICML-W 2026
    GlotOCR Bench: OCR Models Still Struggle Beyond a Handful of Unicode Scripts
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Jana Diesner, and 2 more authors
    In ICML 2026 Workshops: Culture x AI: Evaluating AI as a Cultural Technology; GlobalSouthML (Affinity Event), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib Code Demo

    OCR evaluation has focused on a handful of high- and mid-resource scripts. GlotOCR Bench tests OCR across 100+ Unicode scripts with clean and degraded rendered images. Most vision-language models do well on fewer than ten scripts, and even the strongest fail beyond thirty; performance tracks pretraining coverage, and on unfamiliar scripts models output noise or hallucinate characters from similar scripts.

  4. WWW 2026
    GlotWeb: Web Indexing for Minority Languages
    Abdullah Al Sefat, Amir Hossein Kargaran, François Yvon, and 1 more author
    In Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference 2026, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib ACM Code Demo

    GlotWeb is a web indexing system for minority languages. It finds web content in 400+ languages, verifies the language with GlotLID and filters it for quality, yielding 169K+ verified links, 47% of them in languages missing from major multilingual datasets. The pipeline and an interactive demo are open.

2025

  1. arXiv 2025
    Insights from the ICLR Peer Review and Rebuttal Process
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Jing Yang, and 1 more author
    2025
    Abs arXiv Bib Code

    A large-scale analysis of the ICLR 2024 and 2025 peer review, before and after rebuttal. Combining score statistics with LLM-based analysis of reviews and rebuttals, we find that initial scores and co-reviewers’ ratings best predict score changes, and that rebuttals matter most for borderline papers. We derive practical advice for authors and for designing fairer review processes.

  2. EMNLP 2025
    CoBia: Constructed Conversations Can Trigger Otherwise Concealed Societal Biases in LLMs
    Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Amir Hossein Kargaran, and Jana Diesner
    In Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib ACL Code

    LLMs pass standard safety checks yet can still reveal harmful biases in conversation. CoBia builds conversations in which the model appears to have made a biased claim about a social group, then tests whether it recovers and rejects biased follow-ups. Across 11 LLMs and six socio-demographic categories, constructed conversations reliably surface and amplify hidden biases.

  3. EMNLP 2025
    On Relation-Specific Neurons in Large Language Models
    Yihong Liu, Runsheng Chen, Lea Hirlimann, and 6 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib ACL Code

    Do some neurons in LLMs encode a relation itself, independent of entities? Studying Llama-2 with a statistics-based method, we find relation-specific neurons and show three properties: cumulativity (many neurons jointly encode a relation), versatility (neurons are shared across relations and even languages), and interference (deactivating one relation’s neurons can improve recall for others).

  4. EMNLP 2025
    Tracing Multilingual Factual Knowledge Acquisition in Pretraining
    Yihong Liu, Mingyang Wang, Amir Hossein Kargaran, and 5 more authors
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2025, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib ACL Code

    We trace how multilingual factual recall and crosslingual consistency develop during pretraining, using OLMo-7B. Both improve over time, driven mainly by how often a fact appears in the corpus. Some rare non-English facts are still recalled thanks to crosslingual transfer from English, an effect that emerges early in pretraining.

  5. COLM 2025
    FineWeb2: One Pipeline to Scale Them All – Adapting Pre-Training Data Processing to Every Language
    Guilherme Penedo, Hynek Kydlı́ček, Vinko Sabolčec, and 7 more authors
    In Second Conference on Language Modeling, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib Code Demo

    Training strong multilingual LLMs is hard because filtering and deduplication pipelines are difficult to tailor to many languages. We introduce a FineWeb-based curation pipeline that adapts automatically to any language, validate it with extensive ablations, and add a principled rebalancing method. Scaled to 1,000+ languages, it produces FineWeb2, a 20TB (5B-document) multilingual dataset.

  6. ACL 2025
    How Programming Concepts and Neurons Are Shared in Code Language Models
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Yihong Liu, François Yvon, and 1 more author
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2025, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib ACL Code

    How do LLMs represent multiple programming languages (PLs) relative to English? Decoding intermediate layers during translation across 21 PL pairs shows a concept space closer to English. Language-specific neurons concentrate in the bottom layers, PL-exclusive neurons in the top layers, and PLs aligned with many others have no identifiable specific neurons.

  7. ACL 2025
    MEXA: Multilingual Evaluation of English-Centric LLMs via Cross-Lingual Alignment
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Ali Modarressi, Nafiseh Nikeghbal, and 3 more authors
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2025, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib ACL Code Demo

    MEXA estimates the multilingual ability of English-centric LLMs without task benchmarks: it measures how well each language aligns with English in the model’s intermediate layers using parallel sentences. MEXA’s scores correlate strongly with downstream tasks (average Pearson 0.90 across nine models), making it a reliable, low-cost way to evaluate many languages.

  8. SIGTYP 2025
    Construction-Based Reduction of Translationese for Low-Resource Languages: A Pilot Study on Bavarian
    Peiqin Lin, Marion Thaler, Daniela Goschala, and 4 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 7th Workshop on Research in Computational Linguistic Typology and Multilingual NLP, 2025
    Abs Bib ACL Code

    LLMs translating into low-resource languages tend to produce translationese. We train on synthetic data in which a construction unique to the target language is made more frequent. For Bavarian, the model learns the construction and native speakers judge its translations as more natural.

  9. COLING 2025
    How Transliterations Improve Crosslingual Alignment
    Yihong Liu, Mingyang Wang, Amir Hossein Kargaran, and 6 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 31st International Conference on Computational Linguistics, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib ACL Code

    Post-aligning multilingual models on original and transliterated text improves crosslingual alignment, but why? Training models for Polish-Ukrainian and Hindi-Urdu, we find that transliterations alone raise overall similarity and that contrastive transliteration objectives help distinguish matched from random pairs. Better alignment, however, does not always improve downstream performance.

  1. NeurIPS 2024
    GlotCC: An Open Broad-Coverage CommonCrawl Corpus and Pipeline for Minority Languages
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, François Yvon, and Hinrich Schütze
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (Datasets and Benchmarks Track), 2024

  2. ACL 2024
    MaskLID: Code-Switching Language Identification through Iterative Masking
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, François Yvon, and Hinrich Schütze
    In Proceedings of the 62nd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 2: Short Papers), 2024
    Abs arXiv Bib ACL PDF Code Demo

    Sentence-level language identifiers usually return only the dominant language of a code-switched sentence. MaskLID is a training-free method that masks the features of the dominant language so the identifier can detect the second language in the next round, using the LID model itself. We apply it to GlotLID and OpenLID.

  3. LREC 2024
    GlotScript: A Resource and Tool for Low Resource Writing System Identification
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, François Yvon, and Hinrich Schütze
    In Proceedings of the 2024 Joint International Conference on Computational Linguistics, Language Resources and Evaluation (LREC-COLING 2024), 2024
    Abs Oral Presentation arXiv Bib ACL Code Slides

    GlotScript is an open resource and tool for writing system identification. GlotScript-R lists the attested scripts of 7,000+ languages, and GlotScript-T identifies all 161 Unicode 15.0 scripts in text (ISO 15924). We show its use for cleaning multilingual corpora and for analyzing how well language model tokenizers cover low-resource scripts.

  4. MSR 2024
    GIRT-Model: Automated Generation of Issue Report Templates
    Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Amir Hossein Kargaran, and Abbas Heydarnoori
    In 21st IEEE/ACM International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR), Apr 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib ACM Code

    Issue report templates (IRTs) help manage GitHub issues but are rarely adopted. GIRT-Model is a T5-based assistant, instruction-tuned on our GIRT-Instruct dataset, that generates IRTs from a developer’s instructions. It outperforms general models in automatic and human evaluation, and user-study participants found it useful.

2023

  1. EMNLP 2023
    GlotLID: Language Identification for Low-Resource Languages
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Ayyoob Imani, François Yvon, and 1 more author
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2023, 2023
    Abs arXiv Bib ACL PDF Code Poster Demo

    No language identifier combined wide low-resource coverage, reliability and efficiency. GlotLID-M identifies 1,665 languages and outperforms CLD3, FT176, OpenLID and NLLB when balancing F1 and false positive rate. We also analyze the specific challenges of low-resource LID, such as wrong corpus metadata, leakage from high-resource languages and closely related languages.

  2. ACL 2023
    Glot500: Scaling Multilingual Corpora and Language Models to 500 Languages
    Ayyoob ImaniGooghari, Peiqin Lin, Amir Hossein Kargaran, and 8 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 61st Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 1: Long Papers), 2023
    Abs Area Chair Award Oral Presentation arXiv Bib ACL PDF Code

    Instead of making LLMs better for  100 languages, we scale them horizontally. We build Glot500-c, a corpus for 511 mostly low-resource languages, and continue pretraining to create Glot500-m, which improves substantially over XLM-R on five tasks. Representation quality depends on a mix of factors, including corpus size, script, related languages and model capacity.

  3. MSR 2023
    GIRT-Data: Sampling GitHub Issue Report Templates
    Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Amir Hossein Kargaran, Abbas Heydarnoori, and 1 more author
    In IEEE/ACM 20th International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR), 2023
    Abs arXiv Bib IEEE PDF Code

    GitHub introduced issue report templates (IRTs) in 2016, yet only about 5% of repositories use them, and research on them is limited. GIRT-Data is the first and largest dataset of IRTs in YAML and Markdown, covering 1,084,300 repositories (50,032 with IRTs), released with an open-source crawler.

2022

  1. AACL 2022
    Hengam: An Adversarially Trained Transformer for Persian Temporal Tagging
    Sajad Mirzababaei, Amir Hossein Kargaran, Hinrich Schütze, and 1 more author
    In Proceedings of the 2nd Conference of the Asia-Pacific Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics and the 12th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing (Volume 1: Long Papers), 2022
    Abs Bib ACL PDF Video Code

    Hengam is an adversarially trained transformer for Persian temporal tagging. We build a rule-based tagger to weakly label a large, diverse Persian corpus, train a transformer that generalizes beyond the rules, and release HengamGold, the first high-quality gold standard for the task. Hengam reaches a type F1 of 95.42 and handles ambiguity and misspellings.

  1. WebSci 2021
    Wide-AdGraph: Detecting Ad Trackers with a Wide Dependency Chain Graph
    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Mohammad Sadegh Akhondzadeh, Mohammad Reza Heidarpour, and 3 more authors
    In 13th ACM Web Science Conference, 2021
    Abs Best Student Paper DOI arXiv Bib ACM PDF_V2 PDF_V1 Video Code

    Ad and tracker blocking relies on crowd-sourced filter lists that are hard to maintain. We build a large graph connecting resource requests across many websites and train a classifier on its features to detect ads and trackers. On the Alexa top-10K sites it is accurate and robust to evasion, and it finds services missing from existing lists.

  2. ADCHEM 2021

    Amir Hossein Kargaran, Amir Neshastegaran, Iman Izadi, and 1 more author
